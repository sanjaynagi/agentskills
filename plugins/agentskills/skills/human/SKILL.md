# Human

Two failure modes, one fix: writing that's needlessly hard to read (bureaucratic prose) and writing that sounds like a machine wrote it (AI slop). Both come from the same habit - reaching for the inflated version of a sentence instead of the plain one. Fix them together.

## Before rewriting, ask

Who reads this, and what do they need to do or know after? Plain English is audience-relative - a passage plain to a specialist can be opaque to a layperson. Establish this (or assume and flag it) before touching a word.

## The moves that do most of the work

- **"You," active voice, common words.** "You may apply," not "applicants may apply." "We sent it," not "it was sent." "Use," not "utilize." "Help," not "facilitate."
- **Verbs, not nominalizations.** "Decide" beats "make a decision."
- **One idea per sentence. Vary the length.** Short sentences for the load-bearing points. Don't let every sentence land at the same mid-length cadence - that evenness is itself a tell.
- **Front-load.** The point goes near the top of the paragraph/section, not the end.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Write the date on the form," not "indicate the temporal coordinates."
- **Cut, don't pad.** If a phrase can go without losing meaning, it goes.

## Hard-cut list (near-automatic deletions)

- **Em/en dashes (—, –) and `--`.** Replace with a period, comma, colon, or parentheses. Zero tolerance - scan the final draft for `—`/`–` before delivering.
- **AI vocabulary:** delve, crucial, intricate, tapestry, landscape (abstract), testament, underscore(s), pivotal, garner, foster, showcase, boast(s), vibrant, nestled, in the heart of.
- **Significance-inflation:** "stands as a testament to," "marks a pivotal moment," "underscores its importance," "reflects broader trends." Say the plain fact instead.
- **Vague authorities:** "experts believe," "industry observers note" without a named source. Cite the source or cut the claim.
- **Superficial -ing tails:** "...highlighting its role," "...fostering collaboration" bolted onto a sentence for fake depth. Cut the tail.
- **Rule-of-three padding & elegant variation.** Don't force triads; don't cycle synonyms for the same referent (protagonist/hero/main character/central figure - pick one).
- **Copula avoidance:** "serves as," "stands as," "boasts" instead of plain "is"/"has."
- **Negative parallelisms & tailing negations:** "not just X, it's Y"; "...no guessing" tacked on. Write the real clause.
- **Filler & hedging:** "in order to," "due to the fact that," "it is important to note that," "could potentially possibly." Cut to the claim.
- **Weasel words and doublets:** "where appropriate," "any and all," "null and void" - pick one word, or name the actual contingency.
- **Formatting tells:** title-case headings, emoji bullets, bold-header vertical lists ("**Security:** Security has been..."), curly quotes in plain text, throat-clearing right after a heading ("Speed matters." then the real point).
- **Chatbot residue:** "I hope this helps!", "Let's dive in," "Great question!" - delete; it's not content.
- **Generic upbeat closers:** "the future looks bright," "exciting times ahead" - either state the concrete next fact or end the piece.
- **Formulaic "Challenges and Future" sections** and knowledge-cutoff/speculative filler ("not publicly available, suggesting she keeps a low profile") - state what's known, or cut the section.

## Don't over-correct

Not every instance of these patterns is AI-written. Perfect grammar, formal vocabulary used precisely, a single "however," curly quotes from an auto-formatter, one em dash from a human editor - none of these alone is evidence. Look for **clusters**. And preserve real human signal when you see it: specific hard-to-fabricate detail, mixed feelings left unresolved, genuine asides, uneven sentence rhythm. Don't flatten those chasing "clean."

This calibration is content-dependent: technical, legal, or reference writing should stay neutral and plain - don't inject opinion or first-person voice there. For essays, blogs, or personal writing, a flat, opinion-free, evenly-paced draft is itself a tell; add a real reaction, let a tangent through, vary the rhythm.

## Process

1. Establish the audience and task (or state your assumption).
2. Draft the rewrite - plain-English moves applied, hard-cut list removed.
3. Re-scan the draft once for what's *still* obviously AI or bureaucratic. Fix those specifically.
4. Deliver the rewrite. Note the 3-5 highest-leverage changes made, briefly - skip a line-by-line log.

Keep the rewrite the same length as the original's actual content (don't drop coverage), but expect it to run shorter, since most of what gets cut was padding.
